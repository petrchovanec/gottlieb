## 1. Evidence rules

The project distinguishes evidence from interpretation and presentation.

- **verified-direct** — the source or a sufficiently detailed source record was inspected.
- **prior-chat-verified** — inspected previously, but the scan or page should be reacquired.
- **catalog-verified** — source existence and bibliographic identity are verified; contents are not.
- **official-secondary** — an official or archival institution's historical account was inspected, but its underlying primary files were not.
- **user-supplied** — supplied through family or genealogical research and not independently established by that item alone.
- **indexed-lead** — indicated by an index, listing, catalog, or OCR result; the underlying primary item was not adequately inspected.
- **needs-recheck** — an earlier finding lacks enough current documentation for publication as fact.
- **conflict** — evidence conflicts with another source and no explanation has been proved.
- **research-lead / high-priority-research-lead** — a source or collection to investigate, not proof of a biographical claim.
- **open-question** — unresolved and not a factual assertion.

No source status is silently promoted. Similar names and initials are not merged without person-level corroboration.

## 2. Current bottom line

The project now has three strongly compatible but still distinct evidence layers:

1. A Vienna parish register records a child named **Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb**, born **8 December 1871** and baptized **10 December 1871** at **St. Johann Nepomuk**.
2. Contemporary Zürich sources identify **S. H. Gottlieb** as `Direktor` of the **Savoy-Hotel Baur en Ville** in 1914 and list him as director again in 1922.
3. A contemporary Baden-Baden notice dated 20 March 1931 identifies **Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb** as a hotelier and **Pächter**—lessee or operator—of **Hotel Meßmer**.

The exact full name, compatible initials, shared profession, and plausible chronology make it strongly likely that these records concern the same man. No municipal, court, registration, or other person-level document yet directly connects the Vienna baptismal identity to both hotel careers. The three records must therefore remain separate in structured data until that link is established.

**Evidence status:** verified-direct for the register entry, with corroboration from the supplied derivative index.

The index additionally reports paternal grandmother **Antonia Gottlieb**, maternal grandfather **Josef Leschinger**, and maternal grandmother **Theresia Jakesch**. These names are retained as transcription leads rather than public biographical facts until the handwritten parentage clauses are formally transcribed.

The original image also appears to contain residence, occupational or social-status language, godparent information, and marginal annotations. Those details have not been reliably transcribed and must not be guessed.

### Source limitations

### Identity limitation

The baptism establishes the Vienna child. It does not record Zürich, Baden-Baden, the hotel profession, or any later residence. It materially strengthens the working identity but does not close it.

## 4. Savoy-Hotel Baur en Ville, Zürich

### 1911 lead

Earlier research found a 1911 advertisement apparently using the wording `Direktion: S. H. Gottlieb` for Baur en Ville or the Savoy.

**Status:** prior-chat-verified. The newspaper title, issue, page, permanent URL, and image still need to be reacquired.

### 1914 direct evidence

Else Züblin-Spiller's contemporary guide *Über die Bernina nach Mailand*, Hendschels Luginsland, Heft 44 (Frankfurt am Main, 1914), printed page 127, identifies:

> S. H. Gottlieb, Direktor

in connection with the Savoy-Hotel Baur en Ville.

The digitized OCR was inspected and the publication date was corroborated through the Deutsche Nationalbibliothek. A visual comparison with the printed page remains desirable, but the dated employment point is verified-direct.

This evidence establishes Gottlieb as director in 1914. It does not prove that he served continuously from 1911 through 1922.

### Promotional postcard

An Axioart/Darabanth catalog record describes a Baur en Ville promotional card with the wording `Direktion S.H. Gottlieb`.

**Status:** verified-direct as to the catalog description. No exact date should be assigned until the card itself is inspected.

### 1922 direct evidence

Frederick Dossenbach's *How to See Switzerland: A Practical Guide* (1922) lists:

> Savoy-Baur en Ville — S. H. Gottlieb, dir

**Status:** verified-direct.

The same guide lists Zürich's Hotel Victoria under **A. Kummer-Wenger**.

### Unresolved management chronology

A modern Famous Hotels chronology gives:

- Hermann Duffing, 1909–1910;
- `I. Gottlieb`, 1910–1918;
- Fritz Giger, 1918–1926.

This conflicts with the direct 1922 listing of S. H. Gottlieb as director. The 26 August snapshot calls `I. Gottlieb` a probable typographical or transcription error and describes circa 1910 as a probable start date. That interpretation is not sufficiently proved for publication as fact.

Possible explanations include an erroneous secondary chronology, stale 1922 guide information, different or concurrent management roles, or different people. None has been established.

Website-safe wording is:

> S. H. Gottlieb is documented as director in 1914 and was still listed as director in a 1922 travel guide. A secondary chronology assigns `I. Gottlieb` to 1910–1918 and Fritz Giger to 1918–1926; the discrepancy remains unresolved.

### Savoy research collections

Hans Schulthess, *Savoy Hotel Baur en Ville Zürich 1838–1938* (Zürich, 1938), 64 pages, illustrated, is cataloged and digitized by the Wolfsonian–Florida International University:

https://digital.wolfsonian.org/WOLF063701

Its existence and bibliographic identity are catalog-verified. Its relevant pages have not yet been inspected, so it does not currently establish Gottlieb's exact tenure.

The Schweizerisches Wirtschaftsarchiv also provides a substantial digitized Savoy collection through e-manuscripta:

https://www.e-manuscripta.ch/swa/content/titleinfo/3183313

This is a high-priority research collection, not yet a source for a new biographical fact.

## 6. Hotel Messmer, Baden-Baden

### 1929 advertising lead

Several marketplace listings describe 1929 Hotel Messmer advertising ephemera associated with Gottlieb. Multiple listings strengthen the likelihood that such material exists, but seller metadata is not a substitute for the advertisement.

**Current assessment:** indexed lead. It is not yet safe to state as established fact that Gottlieb began at Hotel Messmer in 1929 or held a particular role that year.

### Reported closure in 1930

The Stadt Baden-Baden Gedenkbuch describes Hotel Messmer as already closed since 1930 (`schon seit 1930 geschlossen`). The municipal account was inspected, but its cited archival files were not.

**Current assessment:** strong official-secondary evidence.

### Verified 1931 role

The *Badische Wirtschafts-Zeitung*, Nr. 6, 20 March 1931, page 68, under `III. Konkurse — b) Aufhebungen`, identifies:

> Hotelier Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb, Pächter des Hotels Meßmer, Baden-Baden.

This establishes:

- the full name Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb;
- his location in Baden-Baden;
- his occupation as hotelier;
- his role as `Pächter`, meaning lessee or operator;
- the appearance of a bankruptcy proceeding concerning him in the `Aufhebungen` section by 20 March 1931.

It does not establish ownership of the hotel property and does not state why the proceeding was terminated. `Mangels Masse` or any other reason must not be inferred.

### 1932 and 1934 directory conflict

Earlier research reported Reichs-Telefonbuch listings from approximately 1932 and 1934 associating `S. H. Gottlieb` with Hotel Messmer, possibly using `Inhaber`.

**Status:** prior-chat-verified; exact pages and wording have not been reacquired.

These listings conflict with the retrospective report that the hotel was closed from 1930. They cannot be used as straightforward proof that Gottlieb operated an open hotel through 1934.

Possible explanations include directory lag, retention of a business listing after closure, continued formal association with the property or business name, incorrect directory dating, or an error in the earlier transcription. No explanation has been proved.

Website-safe wording is:

> Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb was documented in 1931 as hotelier and lessee of Hotel Messmer. An official historical account reports that the hotel had closed in 1930. Later directory findings apparently continued to associate S. H. Gottlieb with Hotel Messmer, but their wording and significance remain unresolved.

### Ownership and later archival trail

The strongest contemporary source calls Gottlieb `Pächter`; he should not be described as owner of the Hotel Messmer property.

An official historical account associates the property with the Lassmann property complex. Two distinct Freiburg archival leads may clarify ownership, leases, debts, inventories, or later administration:

- Staatsarchiv Freiburg, **F 165/1 Nr. 98**, `Rückerstattung (Hotel Messmer, Baden-Baden)`, 1949–1952;
- Staatsarchiv Freiburg, **F 196/1 Nr. 3834**, identified through a Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg provenance-research context page.

Neither underlying file has been inspected. Their possible contents are research objectives, not biographical evidence.

It also remains unresolved whether the Jakob Lassmann associated in secondary material with Zürich's Baur en Ville was the same person whose family was connected with Hotel Messmer. Even if confirmed, that would not prove that Lassmann arranged Gottlieb's move.

## 9. Website-safe working biography

> A Vienna parish register records a Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb born on 8 December 1871 and baptized at St. Johann Nepomuk two days later. Contemporary Zürich sources identify an S. H. Gottlieb as director of the Savoy-Hotel Baur en Ville in 1914 and again list him as director in 1922. In Baden-Baden, a contemporary notice dated 20 March 1931 identifies hotelier Sylvester Hermann Gottlieb as Pächter—lessee or operator—of Hotel Meßmer. The names, initials, profession, and chronology strongly support a single career, but no person-level record yet directly links the Vienna baptismal identity to both hotel records. A reported connection with Zürich's Grand Hotel Victoria remains unverified. Hotel Messmer was reported retrospectively as closed since 1930, creating an unresolved conflict with later directory findings. Gottlieb's later life and death remain unknown.

This biography must retain the identity caveat until a municipal registration card, court file, or equivalent record supplies direct linking information.
